THE MATHEMATICS

Every point of light is a root of E₈: one of 240 vectors in ℝ⁷ that build the largest exceptional Lie group, its 4₂₁ polytope and its Weyl group of 696,729,600 symmetries.

Φ(E₈) = permutations of (±1, ±1, 0⁶)  ∪  ½(±1)⁷ with an even number of minus signs

The Coxeter element w is the product of the eight simple reflections. For E₈ its order is the Coxeter number h = 30, and its eigenvalues are exp(2πim/30) for the exponents m = 1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29.

This page watches ℝ⁷ through a single moving 2-plane. When that plane is the Coxeter plane — the eigenplane of exp(2πi/30) — w spins it by 2π/30, so the orbit of every root lands on a perfect regular 30-gon: 240 = 8 × 30 concentric rings. That is the bloom at rest.

Your pointer steers the viewing plane through the maximal torus T⁴ ⊂ SO(8): four simultaneous rotations in four orthogonal coordinate planes. A generic plane breaks the symmetry into eight-dimensional epicycles; only the Coxeter plane restores all thirty arms at once.

The resonance gauge measures the mean squared gap between each root’s image w·α and the in-plane rotation of α by 2π/30. It reads 100% if and only if the plane you are looking through is the Coxeter plane. Stir, wander — then find your way back.
